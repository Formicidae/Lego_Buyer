Where to buy

Compares LEGO against marketplace prices (BrickLink price guide, also used as the BrickOwl estimate).

Download BrickLink wanted list

Pick a Brick format: on lego.com open Pick a Brick → Upload list and choose the file (element id and quantity, up to 200 different pieces per file). LEGO puts what it sells in your bag and lists the rest as unavailable.

Cheaper colours for expensive parts

A part that only came in one or two sets can cost many times what the same shape costs in a common colour. This looks up the other colours of every part costing at least the amount below, and writes your picks to a new list; this list is left as it is.

Download this list's price data — one file with every part's prices, the imported carts and how the totals above were worked out; attach it when something here looks wrong.

Import an order you placed (mark as owned, sorting sheet)

After ordering on BrickOwl, open the order (Order History → the order), save that page (browser menu → Download page, an .mht or .html file) and choose it here. Each lot is matched to what this list still needs; with several lists open together, pieces go to the lists in their order. Then mark everything as owned in one tap, and print the sheet that says which bag goes to which set.

Import a LEGO Pick a Brick bag

For LEGO's real prices and what it actually stocks: download the Pick a Brick file for the whole list above, upload it on lego.com (Pick a Brick → Upload list; with several files, upload them one after another), then save that page with the bag showing (browser menu → Download page, an .mht or .html file) and choose it here. Every piece in the bag gets LEGO's price; the plans above then show what is worth buying there, and Pick a Brick file: LEGO side of this plan is the list of just those pieces.

Import a BrickOwl cart

On BrickOwl's Buy Wishlist page, tick the stores you want, then save the page (Ctrl/⌘-S → “Webpage, Single File” .mht, or “Complete” .html) and upload it: every allocated lot's price is read and shown on the part cards as BO, and the marketplace scenarios use those real prices. Pasting the page text or a printed PDF also works, but only gives store totals. Each configuration becomes a scenario above.
